# Creative rules

## Hooks belong to the captain

Do not write new hooks, rewrite his hooks, or supply AI-made alternatives. Use only hooks the captain wrote, copied exactly from his message or `core/approved-hooks.md`. Cleaned historical lists and third-party frameworks are inspiration, not approved wording. If a concept has no suitable captain-written hook, leave it blank for him. Why: the captain rejected the agent-written hooks and said AI is the worst at hook writing.

## Creative briefs stay creative

When the captain asks for a template, provide an editable document with blanks for his idea, audience, situation, emotion, exact hook, sequence, app moment, proof and reference. Do not decide the batch size, format mix, deadlines or CTA for him. He describes the concept; the agent makes the instructions clear without inventing a hook or changing his intent. Why: he wants a template he can describe things in and fill, not an agent-authored campaign.

Keep payment, pricing, contracts and account administration out of a creative-only template unless the captain requests them. When he asks for payment, usage rights or other deal details, include a concise separate terms page in the same creator packet. Mark unagreed terms plainly instead of inventing a fee, rights grant or deadline. Why: filming instructions should stay simple, while a requested commercial handoff must not hide the deal.

## Product truth

Use `core/product.md` for the product purpose, language and distinct unlock modes. Keep the fitness goal central. A single-condition demonstration does not make ExcerLock a single-condition product.

Use “apps” or “selected/chosen apps”; avoid “scroll” and “scrolling” in official copy. Preserve captain-written copy exactly.

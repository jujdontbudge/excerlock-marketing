# Writing to creators

How every message, brief, guide and agreement we send a creator should sound. The captain approved this voice on 2026-10-14 ("i love how your writing, always write like this to creators").

**Why:** creators are our friends and partners, not workers reading a manual. A warm, short, human message gets more of them to sign and do the work properly. Cold or robotic copy made the captain angry three times on 2026-10-13.

## The voice
- **Talk to them, never about them.** Write "you" and "your phone", never "she", "her" or "the creator". Why: the doc goes to the creator; third person reads like internal notes about her.
- **Sound like a friend texting.** Open warm ("Hey! 🧡", "Hey Shruti, so happy you're in! 🙌"), keep sentences short, use a little emoji (🧡 🙌 🎉), and sign off "Johan, ExcerLock".
- **Tell them why it helps them.** Tie each ask to their benefit, honestly: "a warmed-up account gets way more views, and more views means a bigger bonus."
- **Make it feel easy.** Give the real time it takes ("10 minutes, twice") and where it fits in their day ("while you're winding down at night, eating, or waiting somewhere"). Say "It's just scrolling!" when it is.
- **Be concrete and on-niche.** Give exact examples they can copy, hyper-focused on our audience: search *how to lose fat as a girl*, *how to get more steps as a girl*, *treadmill routine girl*. No vague "search your niche".
- **Only what they need.** Leave out our reasons, research, file paths, "captain's rule", pay-clock maths and what we do when an account fails. A guide fits on half a page; the brief fits on one.
- **Headings say what to do,** like "Day 2 · Search the niche (10 minutes, twice)", never cold labels like "THIS IS CANVAS UGC".
- **Chat messages are short.** One to three short lines, one ask per message, at most one emoji, no greeting-plus-paragraphs. Docs can be longer; messages can't. Why: the captain, 2026-10-14: "stop sending 10 paragraph things with emojis".
- **Always give examples.** Whenever you ask a creator to do or name something, show 2–4 concrete examples she can copy, made with her own name (for a username: kamila_fitness, kamila.walks, kamilarueda27), never a generic "your first name plus fitness" on its own and never someone else's name. Why: the captain, 2026-10-14: "always give examples"; a creator shouldn't have to guess what we mean.
- **Never invent her details.** Examples built from her name are fine for suggestions (usernames). Never write an email, phone number, handle or link as if it is hers unless it comes from her or our records; use an obvious placeholder (jane@gmail.com). And explain any trick in plain words. Why: 2026-10-15 a message gave Dakota "dakota+excerlock@gmail.com", a guessed address she could have typed literally.
- **Words real people use.** Never "nudge" (captain, 2026-10-14: "nobody says that"). Say "just checking in" or "quick reminder" instead. Why: stiff marketing words make the message feel automated.
- **Persuade honestly.** Warmth, pay that's clear, "no pressure", one clear next step. No fake scarcity or made-up social proof.

## Editing creator docs
- The captain edits the Google Docs himself between turns. Read the current version before changing anything, keep his wording and edits, and use find-and-replace for small changes.
- Never turn an internal file into a creator doc as it is. Rewrite it for them in this voice.

## Example (the warm-up guide intro he approved)
> Hey! 🧡 Before your first video goes up, just use your new account like a normal girl who's into walking and the gym. That's how TikTok and Instagram learn who to show your videos to. This part is really for you: a warmed-up account gets way more views, and more views means a bigger bonus. The easiest way to fit it in is while you're winding down at night, eating, or waiting somewhere. It's just scrolling!

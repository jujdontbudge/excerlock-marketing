# Slideshow formats

The deck shapes from the three articles, each with its slide-by-slide shape, when it fits and the hook types (numbers from `hooks.md`) that lead into it. Slide counts below are the authors'. Our decks keep their own length and CTA slot: H-slide-count and H-cta-last-slide in `sections/slideshows/rules.md`. Fit a format into those slots; don't stretch the slots to fit a format.

The basic unit, per Sulfur: a slideshow is about four to eight still images with text and music, and each slide is one image plus one sentence. The text can be baked into the image or added with TikTok's own text tool.

"Our data" lines come from our 611-slideshow hook study, `data/slideshow-copy-analysis/report.md` (views and saves captured 14 to 18 Sep 2026; not conversions). The authors' figures are their own and were not verified.

## The formats

### 1. AIDA (Pounds)
Shape:
1. Attention: slide 1 stops the scroll.
2. Interest: the next slides make her want to keep going.
3. Desire: she wants the result.
4. Action: one thing to do.

When it fits: always. It is the spine under every other format here. Use it to check a plan: every slide should move her one step closer to acting, and slide 1 carries the attention job alone.
Leads in from: any hook type.

### 2. Content, warmup, product push (Pounds)
Shape:
1. Early slides deliver real content.
2. One slide warms her toward the solution.
3. The product appears after that, never first.

When it fits: value decks where the product is a natural next step after the tips. This is the closest article shape to our house order of value, product aside, more value.
Leads in from: 2, 7, 11, 19.

### 3. Pain funnel (Sulfur)
Shape:
1. Slide 1 shows a specific pain, in the picture and the words.
2. Slides 2 and 3 add more relatable detail of that pain.
3. A later slide brings in the solution softly.
4. The next slides show it working and show the result.
5. The last slide has one clear call to action.

When it fits: products and apps; Sulfur calls it the best format for them. The deck sells the problem before it shows the solution, so the product is never the opening subject.
Leads in from: 5, 8, 12, 14.

### 4. Swap (Sulfur)
Shape:
1. An undesirable option.
2. A better replacement.
3. A short reason why.
4. Repeat with more swaps.
5. The last slide connects to one relevant product or action.

When it fits: lifestyle, health and habit content ("7 no sugar food swaps"), and titles framed as "instead of X, do Y". It earns saves because it works as a reference sheet.
Leads in from: 5, 7, 9, 10.

### 5. Identity (Sulfur)
Shape:
1. Describe the person she wants to become.
2. Short, affirmation-like lines.
3. One consistent visual mood across all slides.
4. The account feels like a mentor or a mirror.
5. Connect to a product that fits that identity.

When it fits: coaching, ebooks and aspirational accounts. Sulfur says it gives fewer huge peaks but a more loyal, aligned audience.
Our data: show the identity, don't announce it. A shown identity ("the position you'll catch us in when we're doom scrolling", 3.2M views) beats an announced one ("for the girls who can never put their phone down", 32k).
Leads in from: 8, 9, 20.

### 6. Quick hit (Sulfur)
Shape:
1. Two or three slides.
2. Little or no teaching.
3. Carried by urgency, a sale or a product that explains itself.
4. Straight to the action.

When it fits: bottom of the funnel, for viewers who already want it. Too short for our slot count; use it only where an account's `## Overrides` allow a shorter deck.
Leads in from: 1, 3.

### 7. Product discovery (Sulfur)
Shape:
1. The deck shows someone using the app, or a screenshot with an obvious result.
2. It does not announce or explain the product.
3. The product has a screen, widget or result that shows its value in under a second, so the screenshot does the explaining.
4. Viewers ask what the app is and feel they found it themselves.

When it fits: any app with a clear visual moment. A viewer who asks for the name has stronger intent than one who was pitched.
Our data: put the app in as a screenshot of a history, not as a sentence. Stated claims in ads earn 0.2 to 0.4 saves per 1k views.
Leads in from: 3, 16, 22.

### 8. Story or curiosity sequence (Sulfur, Pedro Pérez)
Shape:
1. Open with an unresolved event or conflict.
2. Reveal one piece of information per slide.
3. Keep the open question alive across the whole deck.
4. Deliver the twist or payoff near the end.

When it fits: hooks that open a loop. Pedro's version is suspicion, missing information, then a reveal.
Our data: story hooks have a median 285k views; the emotional ones earn comments and shares more than saves.
Leads in from: 15, 16, 17, 24.

### 9. Useful reference sequence (Sulfur)
Shape:
1. Promise immediately useful information.
2. Deliver real substance on every slide.
3. Make the post worth saving and coming back to.
4. Connect the last slide to an aligned action.

When it fits: list and how-to titles. Suspense does little here, so each slide must pay on its own.
Our data: lists earn 18.5 saves per 1k views and how-tos 19.6, about twice the 8.9 median. The best list titles name the reader's situation plus one judgement word ("4 apps that will make you disgustingly productive"). Teasing the last item ("best tip at the end") drops the median to 16,700 views.
Leads in from: 4, 6, 7, 11, 12, 13, 18, 19.

### 10. Repeatable signature series (Sulfur)
Shape:
1. The same recognisable opening every time.
2. The same broad structure behind it.
3. A new subject or image each post.

When it fits: once a format has proven itself on an account and can run as a series.
Leads in from: 20.

### 11. Notification (Pedro Pérez)
Shape:
1. Slide 1 starts a story and does not look like an ad.
2. An app notification appears on slide 2.
3. The notification brings the app into the story naturally.
4. The remaining slides develop the story and the result.

When it fits: apps that send a visible notification or lock screen. Pedro picked it because he had seen it work repeatedly across niches.
Leads in from: 15, 16, 24.

## Ways to get a new format

These are methods for finding a format, not deck shapes.

- **Keep the mechanism, change the scenario (Pedro Pérez).** Keep the psychological structure of a proven deck and move it to new relationships, settings and events. A suspicion-then-discovery format should not become ten near-identical cheating stories.
- **Keep the format, swap the product (Sulfur).** A structure proven for one product can carry another aligned product, audience, season or situation. The format stays recognisable; the product and details change.
- **Turn a winning video into slides (Sulfur).** Look past slideshows. Take the pain point and mechanism of a proven video and rebuild it as a deck, before that structure is common in photo mode.

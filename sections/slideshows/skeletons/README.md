# Slideshow skeletons

Reusable blueprints of TikTok photo-slideshow formats: what each slide does and why it's engaging, without the source post's content.

## How a skeleton is written
A skeleton lets a writer rebuild one proven post's format for our audience (girls into the gym, walking, high steps, fat loss and a dream body). It locks what made the post engaging and frees everything else. Skeletons stay Markdown prose for an agent to reason over, not a machine-readable schema — the agent reading it is the point, not a parser.

- **One source post.** Describe its original slide count faithfully, including two/eight-slide examples. This is a source description, not an approved production exception. Before writing a deck, record the assigned count, hook, image policy and captain-approved exceptions; if the source mechanism cannot survive those constraints, report the conflict rather than silently changing either.
- **One entry per slide,** each with Role, Text, Placement and Image, plus the source image link as evidence. Production references are selected under the current picture rules; source images are not automatic permission to reuse them.
- **No CTA, app, product, or account or character names.**
- **Text is set at one of three levels, and the level is marked on the slide:**
  1. **The hook (slide 1) describes the source mechanism, not approved copy.** Keep the source hook's exact wording as a template and turn only the topic-specific words into typed slots: quote the source line, keep its capitals, connective words and voice, and say what each slot takes (`STOP [activity and duration] TO [outcome]`, `DOs and DON'Ts to [goal]`, `7 ways you know your [state] [predicate]`, `6 [topic] tips for [demographic]`). If the hook speaks to a group of people, that slot must name a specific demographic the viewer identifies as, never a broad one; list options taken from the groups other hooks in `research/tagged-hooks/hooks.md` target that fit our audience. Why: the captain saw an open hook come back as a flat title; the hook's premise and who it calls out are part of the format.
  2. **Uniquely emotional or human lines are copied word for word.** If a slide's text lands because of its exact wording (a feeling, a confession, the joke or twist), the skeleton gives that text verbatim as fixed text, exactly as on the source: same words, spelling, capitals and punctuation. Why: the captain: "you ai model suck at this so legit jsut copy word for word what she said". Only mark a line fixed when its exact wording is genuinely the mechanism — don't invent fixed lines.
  3. **Every other slide is open within the source's linguistic relationship to the hook.** Describe the kind of line and its length, and whether it addresses the viewer, answers a question, gives an instruction or names an experience. Preserve that continuity without prescribing identical sentence formulas or line breaks. When adapting, follow [Corpus-first writing](../skills/write-script/SKILL.md#corpus-first-writing-select-adapt-connect): select a near-fitting bank hook and source-supported slide material; internal niche labels are not viewer-facing language. If a bank hook and the skeleton's rigid template cannot fit naturally together, report the conflict rather than force new wording or an invented premise.
- **Caption space is part of every slide.** For each slide say where the text sits and what part of the image must stay clear (a wall band above the head, a dark lower third, a top strip for a label). The image step picks a reference with this space free; a slide with no stated caption region produces images with no room for words.
- **Keep it portable.** Don't carry over the source's topic unless the topic itself is what makes the slide engaging. Many sources are about things outside our audience.
- **Don't claim effects you can't see in the post,** such as "the scariest sign" or "makes people comment". Describe what the slide does and why it reads well; the only evidence is the source post's own views/saves, not our deck.
- **Image honesty.** Where a slide needs something a fictional account can't truthfully produce (a real before/after, a named study), say so and name the honest option (a genuine earlier photo of the character, a real study, or skip the format) instead of leaving it to be faked.
- **A skeleton is draft until a deck built from it passes.** Building one deck from it, reviewing the images and getting the captain's approval is what turns a blueprint into a proven format. `joke-tip-list-twist` is the only proven one so far; the other four are unproven drafts.
- **Sections, in order:** Structure, Slide count, Slide by slide, Hook, Images, Text style, Fixed vs open, Why it works, Porting, Source slideshow (the post link and every source slide image in order).

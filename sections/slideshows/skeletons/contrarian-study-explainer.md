# Contrarian study-backed explainer

**Structure:** a "stop doing X" command against a common habit, a promise of a shorter method backed by a study, the method, the proof, the benefits, then a short all-caps recap.

## Slide count
8: hook, promise, method, proof, 2 benefits, recap, quiet sign-off. Source: [@brownricebandit, 8 slides](https://www.tiktok.com/@brownricebandit/photo/7626326802511727886) (354.7K views, 5.8K saves).

All slides are 4:5 and share one look: polished, stock or AI-style golden-hour photos with mist and a warm, desaturated grade. Every slide carries a small "N/8" page counter (white in a black circle) at the bottom-right corner. Text is always white, centred, with a faint soft shadow and no outline. Positions below are measured from the top of the frame.

## Slide by slide

### Slide 1: contrarian command
- **Role:** tells the viewer to quit a habit they're proud of, which opens a "then what?" gap.
- **Text (template, rigid):** `STOP [activity and duration] TO [outcome]`, all caps. Source-verified: `STOP WALKING AN HOUR A DAY TO LOSE FAT`. Keep the capitals and the word `TO`; only the two slots change. `[activity and duration]` is the common habit stated with its time (walking an hour a day, doing cardio every morning); `[outcome]` is the goal (lose fat, build your dream body). 8-10 words.
- **Placement:** centred, middle, large bold caps, over a tiny distant figure.
- **Image:** wide misty landscape path with a tiny lone figure. Reference: https://www.tiktok.com/@brownricebandit/photo/7626326802511727886 slide 1.

### Slide 2: the promise
- **Role:** less effort, backed by a vague study, while still hiding the method.
- **Text:** 18-22 words.
- **Placement:** centred, lower third, medium.
- **Image:** foggy tree avenue with a tiny distant figure. Reference: https://www.tiktok.com/@brownricebandit/photo/7626326802511727886 slide 2.

### Slide 3: the method
- **Role:** the whole method, simple enough to try today.
- **Text:** the method in short commands with numbers. 18-22 words.
- **Placement:** centred, middle, medium.
- **Image:** legs and shoes from behind, mid-stride on a trail. Reference: https://www.tiktok.com/@brownricebandit/photo/7626326802511727886 slide 3.

### Slide 4: the proof
- **Role:** the study result, for authority.
- **Text:** 20-24 words. The study must be real or honestly attributed: name the population, the comparison and the timeframe in plain words. Don't invent a study for authority; if there's no real one, this format doesn't fit the topic.
- **Placement:** centred, middle, medium.
- **Image:** close shot of a wrist wearing a tracker beside a path. Reference: https://www.tiktok.com/@brownricebandit/photo/7626326802511727886 slide 4. **Note:** the account's no-watch rule overrides this image — use another body-detail shot (a hand on a fence, shoes at a gate), not a tracker.

### Slide 5: benefit 1
- **Role:** something the viewer will feel in their own body.
- **Text:** 18-22 words.
- **Placement:** centred, middle, medium.
- **Image:** backlit legs walking through a field at sunrise. Reference: https://www.tiktok.com/@brownricebandit/photo/7626326802511727886 slide 5.

### Slide 6: benefit 2
- **Role:** a second, invisible benefit in plain words, so it feels scientific without jargon.
- **Text:** 18-22 words.
- **Placement:** centred, middle, medium.
- **Image:** sunrise still life on a bench, heavy bokeh. Reference: https://www.tiktok.com/@brownricebandit/photo/7626326802511727886 slide 6.

### Slide 7: recap
- **Role:** the screenshot line: the whole method in one line, echoing slide 1.
- **Text:** all caps, 8-10 words.
- **Placement:** centred, middle, large bold caps as on slide 1.
- **Image:** golden-hour path with a small figure walking away. Reference: https://www.tiktok.com/@brownricebandit/photo/7626326802511727886 slide 7.

### Slide 8: sign-off
- **Role:** a quiet reason to save.
- **Text:** 5-7 words.
- **Placement:** centred, middle, medium, the poster's handle small below.
- **Image:** close-up of a natural texture with leaf shadows, no subject. Reference: https://www.tiktok.com/@brownricebandit/photo/7626326802511727886 slide 8.

## Hook
A blunt command in the voice of an authority who has read the research. Second person, confident, contrarian; not rude, not a friend.

## Images
- Polished, stock-style or AI-looking photos with one consistent look across every slide: golden-hour light, mist, soft focus, a warm desaturated grade. **This format is the deliberate exception to the organic-phone-photo default: a consistent styled grade is part of its look. The account's character and no-watch/no-tattoo rules still apply.**
- Subjects are anonymous: a small figure walking away down a path, legs and shoes from behind, a wrist, a still life on a bench. Never a face.
- The images illustrate the text loosely (the activity or a detail of it) and set a calm, wise, "nature and science" mood.
- A small page counter ("3/8") bottom-right adds a magazine feel.
- Reserve caption space: body text sits centred in the middle of the frame, so keep the mid-frame free of busy subject detail; the page counter occupies the bottom-right corner.

## Text style
- Two fonts: hook and recap in a bold condensed all-caps display font, white, centred; body slides in a clean humanist sans, sentence case, white, centred in the middle of the frame.
- Key phrases highlighted in a warm beige tint instead of bold or emoji.
- Full punctuation, full sentences on body slides. No emojis.

## Fixed vs open
- **Fixed:** the slide-1 hook template `STOP [activity and duration] TO [outcome]` (rigid); an opening command to quit a common habit; less effort as the promise; a simple numbered protocol; a head-to-head proof slide; a caps recap echoing the hook; one consistent polished grade and faceless subjects. Slide 8's sign-off is open (no uniquely fixed source wording).
- **Open:** the habit being attacked, the method, the study, the colour grade (as long as it's consistent), the setting.
- **Slide count is locked at 8.** This overrides the house 5-7-slide default; the format needs all 8 beats.

## Why it works
The opening command makes viewers feel caught doing something wrong, which is hard to scroll past. The promise of less effort plus a study gives both hope and permission to believe. A precise, tiny protocol is easy to try and easy to save, and the calm, polished images make it feel trustworthy rather than salesy. (These are readings of the format, not measured effects; the only evidence is the source post's own views/saves.)

## Porting
- "Stop doing 10k steps in one go to lose fat": split walks after meals, with a study-style comparison.
- "Stop going to the gym 6 days a week to build your dream body": a 3-day plan and the research behind it.
- "Stop doing an hour of cardio to burn fat": a short incline-walk protocol with a before/after group result.
- "Stop forcing motivation to stay consistent": a 2-minute start rule backed by habit research.
- "Stop walking at a slow pace to hit your step goal": a pace-change method and what it did for walkers in a study.

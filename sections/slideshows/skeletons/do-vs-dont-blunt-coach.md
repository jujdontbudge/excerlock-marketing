# Do vs don't, blunt coach (one item per slide)

**Structure:** a "DOs and DON'Ts to ___" title over the result, a one-line credibility slide over the "before", one ground-rule slide, then Do / Don't slides in the source's exact order, one item each: a one-word label, a short command, a short reason.

## Slide count
8: title, credibility, ground rule, then Do, Don't, Don't, Do, Don't — that exact order, copied from the source, not "alternating". Source: [@charlesjr_fit, 8 slides](https://www.tiktok.com/@charlesjr_fit/photo/7677448535113108738) (967K views, 20K saves). **Slide count is locked at 8,** overriding the house 5-7-slide default.

Photos are vertical 9:16, except the two food slides at 3:4. All text is white TikTok Classic with a thin black outline or soft shadow, sentence case, full stops. Body text is medium (~3% of frame height); the Do./Don't. label is about 1.5x bigger (~4.5%). Positions below are measured from the top of the frame.

## Slide by slide

### Slide 1: title over the result
- **Role:** the result photo persuades before a word is read.
- **Text (template, rigid):** `DOs and DON'Ts to [goal]`. Keep `DOs and DON'Ts to` exactly (caps and the word `to`); only the goal slot changes. 6-7 words.
- **Placement:** centred, middle, across the waist, clear of the face.
- **Image:** full-body mirror selfie showing the result. Reference: https://www.tiktok.com/@charlesjr_fit/photo/7677448535113108738 slide 1.

### Slide 2: credibility
- **Role:** a short "trust me" over the poster's own before photo; the contrast with slide 1 is the argument.
- **Text:** 1-3 words, open.
- **Placement:** centred, middle, medium.
- **Image:** candid before photo of the same person, unflattering light. Reference: https://www.tiktok.com/@charlesjr_fit/photo/7677448535113108738 slide 2. For a fictional account, generate an earlier, less-fit photo of the same character.

### Slide 3: ground rule
- **Role:** a blunt truth before any advice, so the coach sounds honest.
- **Text:** a short setup line, then a myth busted and the hard fact. 14-18 words.
- **Placement:** centred, lower middle, medium.
- **Image:** candid waist-up gym shot mid-motion. Reference: https://www.tiktok.com/@charlesjr_fit/photo/7677448535113108738 slide 3.

### Slide 4: Do
- **Role:** an easy yes.
- **Text:** "Do." label, a short command, then one line of reason. 14-18 words.
- **Placement:** big label near the top, command in the middle, reason in the lower third.
- **Image:** POV of a hand holding the "good" thing in bright sun. Reference: https://www.tiktok.com/@charlesjr_fit/photo/7677448535113108738 slide 4.

### Slide 5: Don't
- **Role:** calls out a popular rule the viewer may be following, then the reason flips it.
- **Text:** "Don't." label, a short command, one line of reason. 12-14 words.
- **Placement:** same stack as slide 4.
- **Image:** night flash photo of the thing people wrongly cut. Reference: https://www.tiktok.com/@charlesjr_fit/photo/7677448535113108738 slide 5.

### Slide 6: Don't
- **Role:** the image shows the coach doing the very thing, which makes it funny and credible.
- **Text:** "Don't." label, a short command, a reason with a number. 12-14 words.
- **Placement:** same stack as slide 4.
- **Image:** candid gym-floor shot of the person mid-exercise. Reference: https://www.tiktok.com/@charlesjr_fit/photo/7677448535113108738 slide 6.

### Slide 7: Do
- **Role:** the surprise Do: something ordinary beats what people think they need.
- **Text:** "Do." label, a short command, one line of reason. 11-13 words.
- **Placement:** label upper right, command and reason in the lower third.
- **Image:** wide candid shot of an everyday outdoor chore. Reference: https://www.tiktok.com/@charlesjr_fit/photo/7677448535113108738 slide 7.

### Slide 8: Don't
- **Role:** the habit most viewers over-rely on, then what to do instead. Ends on relief.
- **Text:** "Don't." label, a short command, then what to do instead with a number. 16-20 words.
- **Placement:** big label near the top, the rest in the lower third.
- **Image:** candid shot of the person resting on a cardio machine. Reference: https://www.tiktok.com/@charlesjr_fit/photo/7677448535113108738 slide 8.

## Hook
A plain do/don't promise in the voice of a blunt coach who has been there. Second person implied, short sentences, no warmth, no jokes. The authority is the poster's own body, not credentials.

## Images
- All organic phone photos of the poster's own life: mirror selfie shirtless for the result, a candid hallway shot of the before body, candid gym shots mid-action, a POV hand holding food, a plate at night, an everyday chore outside.
- Bright, natural or gym light, except where a darker shot suits a Don't.
- Image and text match literally: the Do image shows the good thing, the Don't image shows the bad thing or the person doing it too much.
- Same person across slides so the result stays in view.
- Reserve caption space: each Do/Don't slide stacks a big label near the top, the command mid-frame, and the reason in the lower third, so keep the top strip and lower third clear of subject detail. The two food slides are 3:4; the rest are 9:16.

## Text style
- Plain TikTok sans, white with a thin black outline; the Do/Don't label larger than the body text.
- Label and command stacked with space between; the reason sits lower on the frame.
- Sentence case, full stops everywhere, no emojis. Numbers as digits.

## Fixed vs open
- **Fixed:** the slide-1 hook template `DOs and DON'Ts to [goal]` (rigid); result on slide 1 and an honest before on slide 2; a ground-rule slide before the list; one item per slide; the label-command-reason stack; the exact Do/Don't order (Do, Don't, Don't, Do, Don't); blunt full-stop sentences; photos match the item literally.
- **Open:** the goal, the ground rule, which items, the settings, slide 2's 1-3-word line. Slide 2 needs a real, honest before photo of the poster; without one this skeleton doesn't fit.

## Why it works
The before and after in the first two slides give instant proof without a word of explanation. The repeated label-command-reason rhythm is easy to read fast and feels decisive. Don'ts call out habits the viewer probably has, which stings, and the reason line immediately offers a simple fix, so it feels useful rather than preachy.

## Porting
- "DOs and DON'Ts to hit 12k steps a day": Do walk after every meal; Don't try to do it all in one walk.
- "DOs and DON'Ts to actually stay consistent at the gym": Do lay out your clothes the night before; Don't wait to feel motivated.
- "DOs and DON'Ts to lose fat without the gym": Do walk on calls; Don't live on the treadmill.
- "DOs and DON'Ts to build your dream body in a year": Do track steps and protein; Don't change programmes every month.
- "DOs and DON'Ts for your first month of walking": Do start at what you can do today; Don't jump to 20k on day one.

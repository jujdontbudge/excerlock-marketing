# Signs list (diagnostic second person)

**Structure:** a numbered "how to tell if you have this" title, then one numbered sign per slide written as a symptom the viewer checks against their own life.

## Slide count
8: a title, then 7 signs. Source: [@babystepsfitness2, 8 slides](https://www.tiktok.com/@babystepsfitness2/photo/7404352814291488032) (1.6M views, 23.8K saves). **Slide count is locked at 8,** overriding the house 5-7-slide default.

All slides are 3:4 (the last is a 3:4 photo inside a 9:16 frame with black bars). Every caption is the same: white bold TikTok Classic with a thick black outline, large (~5% of frame height), centred, sentence case, no end punctuation, no emoji. Positions below are measured from the top of the frame.

- **Placement:** centred, lower middle, over the subject's torso, large.

## Hook
A second-person diagnostic question dressed as a list. Voice is a calm, knowing insider, halfway between friend and authority. Not alarmist, not blunt: it states things plainly and lets the viewer draw the conclusion.

## Images
- **Title slide:** the poster in a casual gym mirror selfie, neutral face, fit but normal. It sets up "someone who has sorted this out".
- **Sign slides:** everyday POV phone photos from the poster's life that don't illustrate the sign at all: hands holding a bowl, fruit in a shop, a plate on a counter. Organic, bright to neutral indoor light, slightly messy backgrounds. The image and text are deliberately unrelated: the photos show a calm, healthy routine, which reads as the unspoken fix.
- Same hand, same kitchen feel across slides, so it looks like one person's camera roll.
- Reserve caption space: the large caption sits centred in the lower third, so keep the lower third of every photo free of hands, food detail and clutter. All slides are 3:4; the last is a 3:4 photo letterboxed inside a 9:16 frame with black bars, and the caption goes over the photo, not the bars.

## Text style
- Plain TikTok sans, white with a black outline, medium-large, centred in the lower-middle third.
- Sentence case, numbered "1." to "N.", no end punctuation, no emojis.

## Fixed vs open
- **Fixed:** the slide-1 hook template `7 ways you know your [state or measure] [predicate]` (rigid) and the locked 7 signs; second-person diagnosis; one sign per slide; signs are recognisable everyday symptoms; no fixes in the list; images from a real-looking daily life rather than illustrations of the sign. Individual sign lines are open — the source has no single wording that must be preserved verbatim.
- **Open:** the hidden state, which signs, the setting of the photos, whether the poster appears after slide 1.

## Why it works
Each slide is a tiny quiz the viewer answers about themselves, so they keep swiping to count their score. Naming a vague, private feeling makes the post feel personal. Leaving out the fix withholds closure, and the calm lifestyle photos hint at the answer without saying it. (These are readings of the format, not measured effects; the only evidence is the source post's own views/saves.)

## Porting
- "7 signs you're not walking enough": always tired by 3pm, stiff hips, can't sleep, restless evenings.
- "7 ways you know your gym routine is about to fall apart": skipping one day becomes three, you "rest" by scrolling.
- "7 signs you're closer to your dream body than you think": clothes fitting differently, craving your walk.
- "7 signs your fat loss has stalled": same number for weeks, constant hunger, low steps on weekends.
- "7 ways you know you've become a walking girl": you choose the long way, you check your steps before bed.

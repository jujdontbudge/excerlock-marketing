## Basic slideshow unit

Sulfur defines a TikTok slideshow as roughly four to eight static images with text and music.
The text can be part of the image or added with TikTok's native text tool.
He describes the production unit as one image plus one sentence.

## Pounds: direct-response structure

### AIDA

1. Attention.
2. Interest.
3. Desire.
4. Action.

Pounds treats the first slide as the main attention event.
Every later slide should move the viewer one step closer to acting.

### Problem-to-funnel structure

Pounds gives this general flow:

1. Create slides around a specific problem affecting a specific person.
2. Send the traffic to a free community or other owned audience addressing that problem.
3. Continue nurturing the audience.
4. Present a relevant offer.

The monetization example is affiliate-focused, but the structural lesson is that the post, audience, follow-up destination, and offer should solve the same problem.

### Content, warmup, product push

Pounds describes a sequence in which early slides deliver content, the following slide warms the viewer toward the solution, and the product appears later rather than leading the post.

### One slideshow, one core desire

A slideshow should focus on one desire, benefit, idea, and action instead of presenting many unrelated benefits or products.

## Sulfur: four primary slideshow formats

### The pain funnel

Sulfur calls this best for products and apps.

1. Slide one presents a specific pain visually and verbally.
2. Slides two and three add more relatable detail.
3. A later slide introduces the solution softly.
4. The following slides show it working and show the result.
5. The last slide contains one clear call to action.

The product is not the opening subject.
The sequence sells the problem before revealing the solution.

### The swap format

Sulfur calls this useful for lifestyle, health, and affiliate content.

1. Present an undesirable option.
2. Present a better replacement.
3. Give a short explanation.
4. Repeat with additional swaps.
5. Connect the last slide to one relevant product or action.

His example category is "7 no sugar food swaps."
He says this format often earns saves because it functions as a reference document.

### The identity format

Sulfur calls this useful for ebooks and coaching.

1. Describe the identity the viewer wants to embody.
2. Use short, affirmation-like statements.
3. Maintain a consistent visual atmosphere.
4. Let the account feel like a mentor or mirror.
5. Connect the audience to a product consistent with that desired identity.

He says this may produce fewer extreme peaks while building a more consistently aligned audience.

### The quick hit

Sulfur describes this as a bottom-of-funnel format.

1. Use two or three slides.
2. Provide little or no education.
3. Depend on urgency, a sale, or a product that explains itself.
4. Move quickly to the action.

## Sulfur: additional working structures

### Product discovery

The slideshow shows a person using an app or includes a screenshot containing an obvious result.
It does not immediately announce or explain the product.
The intended response is for viewers to ask what the product is and feel they discovered it themselves.

### Screenshot-ready product moment

The product has a screen, widget, result, or other visual moment that communicates value almost instantly.
The screenshot itself carries much of the marketing explanation.

### Story or curiosity sequence

1. Open with an unresolved event or conflict.
2. Reveal information one slide at a time.
3. Preserve the unanswered question across the sequence.
4. Deliver the twist or payoff near the end.

### Useful reference sequence

1. Promise immediately useful information.
2. Deliver substantive information on every slide.
3. Make the post worth saving or revisiting.
4. Connect the final slide to an aligned action.

### Repeatable signature series

Use the same recognizable opening and broad structure repeatedly while changing the underlying subject or image.
The repetition becomes part of the creator's identity.

### Convert a winning video into slides

Sulfur recommends looking beyond successful slideshows.
A proven video can be deconstructed and rebuilt as a slideshow before that structure becomes common in photo mode.
The pain point and mechanism are transferred; the original media should not be copied.

### Keep the format and swap the product

A structure proven for one product can be adapted to another aligned product, audience, season, or situation.
The format stays recognizable while the product and details change.

## Pedro Pérez: notification format

Pedro selects a slideshow structure in which:

1. The first slide begins a story without looking like an advertisement.
2. An app notification appears on the second slide.
3. The notification introduces the app naturally inside the story.
4. The remaining slides develop the narrative and result.

He considers this worth testing because he had seen it work repeatedly and across different niches.

## Pedro Pérez: one proven mechanism, many scenarios

Pedro's process keeps the psychological structure while changing the surface story.

For example, a format based on suspicion and a later discovery should not become many near-identical cheating stories.
The same suspense mechanism should be transferred to different relationships, settings, and events.

## Pedro Pérez: weekly batch format

1. Collect five to ten recent examples that are already performing.
2. Identify the shared mechanism.
3. Generate approximately 10 to 15 distinct slideshow concepts.
4. Define the visual requirement for every slide.
5. Generate the full asset batch in one production session.
6. Keep the visual style internally consistent within each slideshow.
7. Organize the finished batch across the week.
8. Measure results and use them to shape the next batch.

## Manual and scaled operating formats

### Manual school

Sulfur describes operators who create posts by hand, add text in TikTok, and publish manually.
They value the native appearance and believe heavily automated posts may be detected or throttled.

### Pipeline school

Other operators generate images and text variations in bulk, assemble assets programmatically, and schedule across many accounts.
They accept a possible reduction in native feel in exchange for much higher testing volume.

### Hybrid approach

Sulfur's synthesis is to automate production and organization rather than "faking humanity."
Assets can be produced in batches while native text, quality control, and final publication remain human-controlled, especially on new accounts.

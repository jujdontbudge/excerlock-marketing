# How we write ExcerLock slideshow scripts (captain's walkthrough, 2026-09-19)

The captain rejected two rounds of agent-written scripts ("not a single good word"). This is his walkthrough, kept verbatim in spirit, with the reason behind each rule. It overrides any earlier research rule where they conflict.

Under each of his rules there is one line marked **Why:** taken from our own research: the 611-post hook study (`data/slideshow-copy-analysis/report.md`), the 319-deck body study (`data/slideshow-body-analysis/report.md`) and the operators' AI-copy study (`data/slideshow-ai-copy-x/report.md`). The rule is his; the line under it is only the evidence that it works for the reader. Where the two disagree, the rule wins.

## What was wrong with ours (and why)

1. **Slides did not deliver what the title promised.** Title "how i get my steps in as a phone addict" means every main slide must be a REASON / WAY she gets her steps in. Ours put statistics and mood lines there. A statistic is not a reason; the reader came for reasons and gets nothing.
   - Why: a saved deck "makes a promise on slide 1, then pays it in identical-shaped instalments"; decks that pay the promise one item per slide save at 33.4 per 1k views against a 13.4 baseline, and on the helpfulness scale vibes/mood slides sit at 7.4 saves per 1k while light tips sit at 36.5 (body study). The reader saves what she can do, not what she is told.

2. **No second curiosity pull after the hook.** The reader needs one more reason to swipe before the value starts.
   - Why: the highest-view shape per slide in the corpus is "text on slide 1, picture that proves it on slide 2" (2-slide decks median 442,550 views), and the body study's rule is "put the proof screenshot early (slide 2) and keep the tips after it" (hook study c.1, body study e.12).

3. **No real value.** The tips were vague. Value slides must carry the single most helpful, organic tip, ideally copied word for word from posts that already went viral.
   - Why: the operators with the strongest claimed results feed the agent several complete recent winning decks plus the audience's own comments and let it reorganise those words rather than invent a voice; a copied tip already carries the niche's dialect and has already been tested on the reader (AI-copy study, practices 2 and 5; the sklandmore tip deck was copied nearly verbatim by another account and still saved at 41 per 1k).

4. **CTA written as ad copy or as "i use excerlock".** Must be phrased like a friend's aside: "use excerlock to...", "use an app like excerlock...", "excerlock blocks...".
   - Why: eight of the operator sources put the product inside the story as the next useful step, never as a pitch ("this happened, so I used this"); in the body study the product slide written like the free slides saves at 95.8 per 1k (Albo) while store-card and slogan wording lands at 0.6 to 1.2 per 1k (Flibbo), and the brand's own first person ("I use Arrow for that") is the weaker of the two voices.

5. **Every slide the same length and the same shape.** Organic decks switch it up: one slide is two words ("heres proof"), the next is a full tip.
   - Why: Pound's third rule in the body study, "the musical phrase, not the metronome": the 20-slide emsriley walks deck keeps its beat but moves and drops the personal note so it never reads as a metronome, and the corpus's 1M+ view tier is made of 1 to 3 word slides while its high-save tier is made of 15-word slides; a deck that mixes both gets both.

6. **No context from slide to slide.** A 13-year-old must be able to follow it.
   - Why: every operator gives each slide one narrative job and makes it earn the next swipe so "the sequence reads like someone telling a story, not a list of interchangeable tips"; Tjay's review checks factual continuity from slide to slide, and Mert's test is "if you hide the logo, is there still a reason to watch" (AI-copy study, practices 3 and 6).

7. **Too many face photos with no organic reference behind them.**
   - Why: Pedro takes about 80% of his app-marketing images from Pinterest because they look like what a person would naturally encounter and share, and several operators say all-AI, visually perfect images read as synthetic and ad-like; in the corpus the native photo hook out-performs the designed one (185,600 vs 118,950 median views) and photo-dump bodies save at 9.7 per 1k (AI-copy study, hook study b, body study c.9).

## The universal structure (for "how i / why you're not / want X but" formats)

- **Slide 1, hook: the captain's title, as written.**
  - Why: his titles already sit in the winning shapes (median hook 7 words, lowercase, spoken, one concrete noun, native text in the top third; hook study e.1, e.2, e.6, e.7); nothing in the research improves on a hook the captain has already chosen, and rewriting it is where the two rejected rounds went wrong.

- **Slide 2, second curiosity pull: proof, a receipt, a before-picture.** Text short and casual ("heres proof", "but then i remembered how i was", "and i KNOW what im talking about"). Often a side-by-side screenshot (steps + screen time). May end with a pointer line at the bottom: "this how i hit 15k steps every day now >>>".
  - Why: "the screenshot of a history (workout heat-map, step total, streak) is the corpus's native proof; it says someone actually did this without a claim" (hook study d.4); the same move is live right now on TikTok: "but TRUST me.." then a 60,238-step screenshot (@vs.secretx, 2.1M) and "and I KNOW what i'm talking about" as slide 2 of a gym list (@gabylrei, 233K).

- **Slide 3, value: the most helpful tip for the title's promise, copied word for word from a high-performing TikTok/IG post where one exists** (e.g. "ALWAYS WALK AFTER MEALS", "get up 10 minutes earlier and walk for 10 minutes"). Found in our bank of 3,961 posts or by fresh TikTok/IG research.
  - Why: tip slides with one concrete action and one line of why are the most portable body in the corpus (numbered tips with a mechanism: 45.7 saves per 1k) and the audience's own wording is what the operators call the "live niche dialect"; a tip that already went viral is a tip the reader has already agreed with (body study c.3, AI-copy study practice 2).

- **Slide 4 (or 5), CTA: one organic aside naming the product.** Pattern: "use excerlock to make your phone block apps if your not moving. i legit cant scroll unless im walking around (works on the treadmill!!)". Never "i use excerlock" as the frame; say "use excerlock", "use an app like excerlock", or "excerlock blocks ...". Add organic touches: parentheses with an explanation, exclamation marks, lowercase, small typos are fine.
  - Why: "the product arrives when the story needs a tool, not at an arbitrary slide number" and the bracketed aside is the corpus's own way of qualifying a tip (13 decks use "(note)" brackets, 24 saves per 1k); product placed on slides 1 to 2 saves at 0.6 per 1k, placed after the reader has a reason to care it saves at 23 to 27 per 1k (AI-copy study section 5, body study e.17, e.19).

- **Then 2 more value slides** (e.g. "spend 10 minutes on the treadmill before and after lifts").
  - Why: 6 to 9 slide decks are where saves peak (24.7 per 1k organic vs 10.2 for 3 to 5 slides), and "the promo bodies that save are complete without the product; the ones that do not are bodies that only exist to reach the product slide" (body study b, i.3). Value after the CTA is what makes the CTA an aside instead of the point.

- **Then one last slide (rule / resolve / payoff).**
  - Why: 60% of decks with a single payoff put it on the last slide; closing on a one-line rule or summary saves at 22.8 to 58.7 per 1k while closing on a bare photo saves at 9.3 and closing on "download" is what only 16% of decks do (body study e.13, hook study e.15).

- **Value phrasing must match the title's frame.** "how i ..." -> reasons and ways. "why you're not ..." -> "your doing X, do Y instead" / "instead of X do Y" / "your doing X". "want X but ..." -> "this is what i did".
  - Why: the corpus writes the instruction in the second person and keeps the first person for proof ("you'll check it in 10 mins" / "I haven't taken a direct flight in years"); the "your doing X" frame is the corpus's myth-then-fact pair ("But walking doesn't burn fats" / "It literally does", 287K) and the "this is what i did" frame is its credentials-then-steps deck (body study e.8, hook study d.7).

- **Statistics only as a payoff, rehook or switch-up, and then they must be genuinely intriguing.**
  - Why: the one statistic deck that works compounds a number the reader can do for her own life ("10,000 steps a day / That's 3,650,000 a year", 1.5M, 30.6K saves) and the lecture versions ("here's what no one tells you", "most people quit before...") all sit under 50K views: "the number lands; the lecture doesn't" (hook study d.5, d.11).

- **Vary slide text length on purpose; do not make every slide the same shape.**
  - Why: same reason as fault 5: the beat is kept by layout and read-time, and the single break (a two-word slide after a full tip, a long line after short ones) is "where the reader feels the deck land" (body study d, principle 3).

- **Face photos: max 2 per slideshow (3 at most).** Every face slide must have an exact organic reference (a real TikTok/Pinterest photo) that we digest the style from, then place our character into that scene with the prompting workflow.
  - Why: the corpus rewards the shown identity over the announced one only when the picture is a real-looking scene ("the position you'll catch us in when we're doom scrolling", 3.2M, 57 shares per 1k), and operators say faceless or AI-perfect image runs read as ads; a digested real photo keeps the imperfections that make it organic (hook study d.3, AI-copy study section 6).

- **Every slide in a script gives: the on-screen text, then the organic reference for the picture (real post/photo URL or a described real image found on TikTok/Pinterest/Google), then our variation to generate.** Screens (steps, screen time, lock screen) use real screenshots; our 15k steps screenshot is in hand.
  - Why: "put the app in as a screenshot, not a sentence" (hook study e.13); 46 of the 54 product-last slides in the body study are a screenshot with at most one line of copy, and operators keep text editable and review the whole sequence on a phone before anything is batched (body study i, AI-copy study practices 4 and 6).

## Operators' practices we keep behind his rules

These come from the AI-copy study and only apply where they do not conflict with the walkthrough.

- **Product as a story consequence.** The app appears after the viewer has a reason to care, as the thing the narrator used, never as the opening. (Pedro, Rick, Tjay, Vincent, Brain, babe, Mert, Lucas.)
- **Copy from several recent winning decks, not one viral first slide.** Compare complete decks and ordinary posts from the same accounts to find the mechanism; the hook alone does not show it. (Pedro, Rick, babe, Mert, Geethika.)
- **Comments as input.** The audience's comments, reviews and search phrases supply the words, objections and follow-up questions; reorganise them, do not invent a voice. (Pedro, Tjay, Vincent, Mert.)
- **Under 12 words a slide, casual, like a real person talking** (Rick's hard cap). The captain's tip slides may run longer when the tip is copied whole; the short slides around them keep the average down.
- **Human review of the whole sequence before anything is posted.** Check promise against payoff, duplicate beats, continuity, where the product arrives, claims, and whether it sounds like the account. (Tjay, Vincent, babe, PostDovo, Geethika, Rick.)

## Image / reference preferences (captain, 2026-09-19 reviews)

Every line carries its reason. Positive checks for reviewing a finished script or image set live in `research/slideshows/reviewer-checks.md`.

- Don't open on phone-in-bed or any non-gym imagery: slide 1 is an aesthetic gym or walking mirror pic holding the phone, because the hook picture is what stops the scroll and a good-looking gym pic is what this audience stops for.
- Don't use anything but real proof on the proof slide (the screen-time reference is @caprilola67 https://www.tiktok.com/@caprilola67/photo/7430596096843320608 slide 3), because a fake or decorative proof slide breaks the trust the rest of the deck spends.
- Don't put a random aesthetic photo under a tip: the picture must match the tip's message (e.g. @savwright https://www.tiktok.com/@savwright/photo/7658869024830328094 slide 3 under a walking tip), because the picture is the context a 13-year-old reads before the words; a mismatch makes the slide make no sense.
- Don't write an image for the CTA slide: it is an Instagram photo screenshot the captain supplies, so the script says only "captain supplies Instagram screenshot", because he picks that picture himself.
- Don't spend a third face: after the 2 faces are used, the treadmill pic is the default gym value image, because more faces read as a staged shoot and the treadmill is the one gym scene that is always organic and always on-message for steps.
- Don't run more than 2 screenshots per deck (the CTA screenshot does not count), because a deck of screenshots stops looking like a person's photos.
- Don't put a face on the morning-walk slide: aesthetic morning-walk photo, no face, because the walk is the point and a face there is a third face.
- Don't keep a filler last slide (a rule, a mood line, a summary): end on the last value slide when the closer adds nothing, because a slide that adds nothing is the slide where the viewer leaves.
- Don't reuse a reference photo across slides or decks (only the screen-time screenshot and the CTA may repeat), because repeats look templated and a templated deck reads as an ad.
- Don't give alternates or backups: exactly one organic reference per slide, pick the best and delete the rest, because the captain won't choose between options; that is the agent's job.
- Don't go past 7 slides (5 to 7, never 8) and don't move the CTA off slide 4, because 8 is too long and the CTA at 4 lands before drop-off.
- Don't put an Apple Watch anywhere, not in a reference photo, not in a prompt, not on our girl; if a reference shows one, find another reference, because that is the captain's call, and the reference is what the generator copies, so a watch in the reference ends up in the image even when the prompt says no watch.
- Don't make a cheat sheet, a list, a Notes screenshot or any slide that lays out the whole process of hitting 10k steps; a slide gives ONE valuable piece that solves part of it (a 20 min walk, 3k steps after dinner), never the whole day, because our app is the cheat: a slide that solves the whole day kills the download.
- Don't use iPhone Notes screenshots at all, because a Notes page is the cheat-sheet format and reads as "here is the whole plan".
- Don't copy from @rrachfitness https://www.tiktok.com/@rrachfitness/photo/7378132607768349985 slide 2, ever, because the captain banned it.
- Don't copy a hook slide's text into a value slot: value text comes from a value slide and must make sense on its own, because a hook line standing alone in the middle of a deck makes no sense.
- Don't put two slides in one deck that say the same thing ("walk, in a different place"); cut one and shorten the deck, because redundancy is the number one AI tell.
- Don't write parentheticals in on-screen text; the only brackets are the captain's own CTA wording as he gave it ("(works on the treadmill!!)", "(infinite steps hack)", "(i use this and its a life hack, legit cant scroll unless im moving)") and his title "(it's not your legs)"; a needed aside is its own short line with !! ("free steps!!!"), in one deck only, because agent brackets read as AI.
- Don't write number-progress talk ("takes me from 9k to 15k"), because nobody talks like that; it sounds like a metric, not a person.
- Don't close on a staged or app-made picture: last slides must look organic, because the last picture is the one that sits under the comments and the share.
- Don't put our girl's phone or the leopard case into a non-face prompt: the case belongs only to face/body shots where she is visibly holding the phone; a non-face slide copies the reference's device and props 1:1 (iPad stays iPad, TV stays TV, no phone added where the reference has none), because the generator forces the case into scene after scene and paints the pattern onto screens and bezels, and a repeated prop is a templated-ad tell.
- Don't treat the leopard phone case as part of her body. Why: the character brief listed the case with her hair and skin, so every face prompt and every attached reference forced a phone held camera-out into the picture, even on a couch (captain, 2026-09-19: "why are you forcing the phone into every pic"). The case is a prop: it appears only in a mirror selfie where the phone's back naturally faces the mirror. A front-camera selfie shows no phone (the phone is the camera); a scrolling scene shows the phone edge-on with its screen toward her; the case reference image is attached only to mirror-selfie calls.
- (image) Inspect at full size: every limb, joint, hand, foot. Knees bend one way, feet point the same way as the leg, shins/thighs/forearms in proportion, five fingers, one of each body part. Any twist, extra bend, missing/duplicated part, wrong-way foot -> FAIL and regenerate. Why: broken anatomy outs the photo as AI instantly and kills trust in the account.
- (process) Image review is ALWAYS a separate reviewer session with the reference beside the image. If the reviewer dies or hits a limit, start a NEW reviewer and wait; the worker never grades its own images and never marks PASS itself. Why: the maker cannot see its own mistakes - this slide passed only because you self-reviewed after reviewer-1 hit its limit.
- Don't review a generated image on its own: the reviewer sees the organic reference next to our image and fails any device swap, added phone or case, pattern rendered on a screen or bezel, or prop drift, because a slide that passes without its reference beside it is exactly how the walking-pad iPad became a leopard phone.
**HARD RULE - her body in every picture, before pictures included (captain 2026-09-20, third time raised)

- Don't ever render her soft, flat, shapeless, or like a beginner gym girl - not in a hook, not in a value slide, and NOT in a "before" slide. Why: the captain has rejected this three times; it makes her look worse than the viewer and the account is selling her body.
- Every picture that shows her body copies the body in `data/slideshow-02-05-plan/body-benchmark-02-s1.png` (deck 02 slide 1): thick toned legs, big chest, tight waist, slim soft arms, no muscle definition, visibly fit. Why: one benchmark image removes interpretation; the reviewer compares to a picture, not to words.
- A "before" slide is told by the setting, outfit, and caption, never by a worse body. Why: the story works fine with the same body; a worse body breaks the rule above and there is no exception. Scope (captain correction 2026-09-20): the benchmark rule applies to CURRENT pictures; before slides are exempt and may look softer/heavier, that is the story (deck 03 slide 2 is exempt; deck 05 slides 1 and 2 both count as current on the captain's call).

- (creation) Attach physique-anchor + benchmark as references and state the body explicitly in every body-visible prompt. For 05 s2 the baggy tee must still show the body (tee tucked/cropped or fitted, shorts showing thick legs) - 'baggy' is not allowed to hide it.
- (arms, captain addition 2026-09-20) Her arms must NOT be muscular at all - no visible biceps, triceps, or shoulder definition. Arms are slim and soft, feminine; any muscular arm is a FAIL. Benchmark comparison: legs/chest/waist from the benchmark; arms slimmer and softer than the benchmark if it shows definition.
- (review) The reviewer puts the benchmark beside every body shot; softer/flatter/slimmer/boxier = FAIL on any current picture; before slides exempt.

- Don't render our girl looking fat, soft, or wiry/flat. Why: the account sells a body result; a girl who looks worse than the viewer makes the slideshow a lie and she scrolls. In every picture showing her body she looks very good: not fat, thick toned legs, big breasts, slim soft arms, no muscle definition, tight waist, real skin (captain, 2026-09-19: "she need to look much better... not fat at all. but thick legs big breast skinner but toned arms").
- Don't use different steps screenshots per slideshow. Why: one real receipt across all slideshows keeps the account consistent and believable. The receipt is `data/slide5-steps/steps-final-16349.png` (Apple Health Steps, M view, AVERAGE 16,349 steps, Jul 30-Aug 29 2026, dark).
- Don't pair 'how i was / before' copy with a steps or stats screenshot. Why: 'how i was' reads as a BEFORE picture of her; a chart there breaks the story.

## Self-check before a script goes to the captain

1. Read the title, then read only the value slides. Does each one literally deliver what the title promised, in the title's frame?
2. Is slide 2 short, casual, and a reason to swipe (proof, receipt, before picture)?
3. Is every value slide a real tip from a real post, cited under the slide?
4. Is the CTA one aside in "use excerlock to..." / "use an app like excerlock..." / "excerlock blocks..." form, with a parenthesis or exclamation mark, and never "i use excerlock" as the frame?
5. Do the slide lengths vary on purpose (a two-word slide next to a full tip)?
6. Could a 13-year-old retell the deck slide by slide?
7. Two face photos at most, each with an exact organic reference and a digest-then-place variation? Screens as real screenshots, at most two per deck? One reference per slide, none repeated, CTA image left blank?
8. No statistic on a value slide; no em dashes; lowercase and casual grammar where the account voice is lowercase.

# UGC briefs: Jake Castillo, Lucas Patiri and Jonny Scales

Research checked October 7, 2026. Focus: creative direction, reference selection, hooks, product demonstrations and feedback. Pricing, contracts and legal material excluded.

## Bottom line

A brief should be **short, but specific enough to film**. Reaction, POV and talking head are production categories—not complete assignments. Each needs a viewer situation, emotional angle, opening, sequence and product payoff.

Keep the full hook/reference bank internal. Send one short common brief plus only the assigned creative cards and references. Prescribe what the video must communicate and which structure is being tested; leave natural wording and performance to the creator.

## What each account contributes

### Jake: clarity without micromanagement

**Direct advice:** Keep briefs short and functional. Let experienced creators integrate the product into the content they already make rather than turning their performance into a scripted commercial. Reverse-engineer hits and repeat the structure.

Sources:

### Lucas: a one-page brief and disciplined execution

**Direct advice:** The brief has five core components:
1. One-sentence objective: what the viewer should believe after watching.
2. Two to four important product messages, not the complete feature inventory.
3. Specific audience insights.
4. Content requirements and required product/branding moments.
5. A small pack of high-performing reference videos.

Default to guidance rather than word-for-word scripts, with more structure for very new creators. Explain the app simply and check that the creator can explain it back. Start with test drafts, give quick feedback, compare winners and losers, and update the next brief from evidence.

Sources:
- [How to Scale a UGC Program From Zero to 2 Billion Views](https://x.com/lucaspatiri_/status/2078211411725975668), sections 2, 4, 7, 9 and 10.
- [10 UGC mistakes that kill app campaigns](https://x.com/lucaspatiri_/status/2047395217972531604), mistakes 1, 3, 4, 5 and 6.
- [How to use ChatGPT Astra for UGC](https://x.com/lucaspatiri_/status/2097357340215279710), prose on brief drift, comment mining, personalized briefs, winner/loser comparison and feedback. Model specifications and campaign percentages are not adopted as verified facts.
- [How to steal viral formats and make them work for your app](https://x.com/lucaspatiri_/status/2090519163769131270): preserve the mechanism and match the audience emotion rather than copying a topic indiscriminately.
- [Your UGC program isn't failing because of creators](https://x.com/lucaspatiri_/status/2100669543060275626): favor repeated testing of a structure over testing many unrelated formats once.

**Interpretation:** The creator-facing brief should say what stays fixed and what can change. Otherwise a shared document can produce unrelated executions and lose its core message. Personalize tone and filming instructions without silently changing the experiment.

### Jonny: the clearest instructions for what actually gets filmed

**Direct advice:** A useful brief identifies audience, specific problem, emotion, narrative, hook, format, talking points, product bridge, actual screen, reference and conversion path. New creators need more direction; proven creators get more freedom.

Build a story from **audience → trigger moment → emotion → desired outcome**. A vague demographic or broad statement such as “people want to exercise” is not a shootable situation.

References teach mechanisms, not just surface imitation. Explain the hook, story, pacing, emotion, product integration and payoff. Talking head should feel like a call to a friend. Feedback should identify a problem, an exact change and the intended effect. Change one major variable at a time when diagnosing performance.

Sources:

- [How to Build a UGC Team for Your App](https://x.com/jonnyscales/status/2092313031371891004), sections 3–6: don't force every creator to do every production task; remove brief ambiguity; fast QA; distinguish attention from product interest.
- [How to Turn Your UGC Creators into App Install Machines](https://x.com/jonnyscales/status/2097326515151077511), sections 2–6: concise brief, skill-dependent freedom, pre-post QA, explain why changes matter, share performance evidence.
- [How to Build a Viral Strategy for Your App](https://x.com/jonnyscales/status/2092609399776768196): distinguish narrative from hook, select formats to fit the narrative, specify the bridge to the app, test variations.
- [10 UGC Hooks That Always Work](https://x.com/jonnyscales/status/2102879882153730442): concrete hook frameworks with an immediate proof/payoff. The title is not evidence that they always work.

## Recommended packet — our synthesis, not a quoted author template

**Common brief, approximately one page**
- What the app does, in two sentences.
- Who is watching and the moment causing the problem.
- What the viewer should understand/believe afterward.
- The few messages this batch must communicate.
- Assignment quantities and which cards apply.
- Approved assets, one next step, first draft instructions.

**One short card per actual concept**
- Viewer situation + intended emotion.
- Format + narrative.
- First frame + hook direction.
- Sequence of the main beats.
- App bridge + exact proof shot.
- One relevant reference + why it was chosen.
- Fixed elements / creator freedom.
- Variation being tested.

A category can contain more than one concept; don't pretend one generic talking-head card covers unrelated stories. Conversely, several hook variants of the same concept do not need separate long scripts.

**Internal only**
- Full hook/reference bank.
- Test hypothesis and comparison plan.
- Performance tracking and notes for the next batch.

## Creative decisions that remain

These are filming decisions, not commercial/legal questions:
1. The primary viewer situation for each concept.
2. The desired belief and emotion.
3. Which selected hook/reference each concept follows.
4. The exact product proof footage available to creators.
5. Whether each reaction is a standalone short reaction or reaction → demonstration. These are different executions, not interchangeable.
6. What stays fixed across variations and what changes.
7. Which creator gets which concept based on actual delivery strengths.
8. The launch-appropriate next step and where the first drafts go.

The current proposed reaction/POV/talking-head mix can stay. Do not require every creator to cover all three just to fill a quota if their test drafts show a much stronger fit for one.

## Resolve the apparent contradictions

- **Jake: let them cook. Jonny: remove ambiguity.** Both can be followed: be strict about the communication and proof, flexible about performance. More structure for unproven creators, less for proven ones.
- **Lucas: one format repeated. Earlier Lucas: test several new formats. Jonny: test broadly, then converge.** There is no universal quota. For this pilot, retain the chosen three categories, repeat actual concepts, and concentrate later output on signals rather than expanding the format list.
- **Lucas: app nearly invisible. Jonny: unmistakable app context. Jake: direct explanation when needed.** Don't turn a sub-three-second product appearance into a rule. Entertainment must lead somewhere understandable; the demo needs enough screen time for this product's mechanism to register.
- **References versus scripts.** Borrow the attention mechanism and beat order, not another creator's entire wording or fabricated personal experience. Tight talking points or a sample script can help a beginner without making every creator sound identical.

Do not import the authors' unsupported account-health thresholds, guaranteed warm-up effects, blanket AI-suppression percentages, attractiveness-performance claims, or “always works” language as proven rules. They are not necessary to make the creative brief better.

## Internal review and feedback

Before approving: does the opening earn attention, does the creator sound natural, does the story lead into the app, is the required proof visible/readable, and can a viewer explain the benefit?

Feedback format: **problem → exact edit → why**. Example instruction, not source quotation: “The pause before the screen reveal stalls the payoff. Cut that pause and show the approved screen on the line explaining access. The viewer should see the cause and effect together.”

Keep the first reaction and POV review. Provide a prompt response rather than adding more approval gates. Use winners and losers to change the next assignment; don't simply send another list of random hooks.

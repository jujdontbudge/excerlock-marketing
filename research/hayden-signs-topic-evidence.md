# Topic Evidence & Corpus Accounting: Hayden's Signs Slideshow

This document records the organic research, topic rationale, actual source records, and sign-by-sign adaptation trace for Hayden's eight-slide diagnostic slideshow (`sections/slideshows/skeletons/signs-list-diagnostic.md`). Both medical/hormonal diagnosis (cortisol) and checkable measurement (low step count, underfueling) approaches have been discarded in favor of a strictly non-medical, behavioral self-test on perfectionism and all-or-nothing friction in fitness.

---

## 1. Audience & Topic Rationale

### Target Audience
- **Demographic:** Fitness girls broadly (18–26) navigating strength training, gym consistency, healthy habits, and lifestyle balance. Walking is a natural everyday element, but not compulsory or the sole focus.

### The Topic: Unnecessary Fitness Difficulty & Perfectionism
- **Hook (Slide 1):** `7 signs you're making fitness harder than it needs to be`
- **Core Premise:** An all-or-nothing perfectionist mindset creates massive unnecessary friction for fitness girls. Instead of sustaining a moderate, repeatable routine, they impose rigid unwritten rules (must have a full hour, must leave exhausted, must have a clean week starting Monday, must have the perfect matching set).
- **Why this works as a behavioral self-test:**
  - **100% Non-Medical:** Zero symptoms, zero endocrine/hormonal diagnoses, zero starvation or metabolic claims.
  - **Not a Directly Measurable Counter:** Unlike checking a step counter or bathroom scale, someone trapped in perfectionism does not recognize that her own rigid mental standards are the obstacle making fitness feel exhausting.
  - **Self-Test Recognition:** Each slide acts as a tiny behavioral mirror where the viewer recognizes an everyday habit she assumed was "normal discipline," discovering it is actually sabotaging her consistency.

## 3. Seven Distinct Behavioral Signs Trace

Each slide captures a separate, specific domain of fitness friction without repeating the same mechanism:

1. **Slide 1: Hook**
   - **Text:** `7 signs you're making fitness harder than it needs to be`
   - **Domain:** Behavioral self-test framing.
   - **Payoff:** Stops the scroll by calling out the unspoken exhaustion of trying to be a "perfect" gym girl.
2. **Slide 2: Sign 1 (Workout Duration / Time)**
   - **Text:** `1. Skipping the gym if you only have twenty minutes`
   - **Behavior:** The arbitrary time-threshold rule where anything under a full 60-minute split is dismissed as pointless.
   - **Payoff:** Instant recognition of an unhelpful all-or-nothing standard.
3. **Slide 3: Sign 2 (Calendar / Scheduling Reset)**
   - **Text:** `2. Writing off the whole week after one missed session`
   - **Behavior:** Missing a single Thursday lift and using it as an excuse to abandon Friday, Saturday, and Sunday so you can "restart fresh on Monday."
   - **Payoff:** Calls out the classic calendar-perfection trap (not repeated in any other slide).
4. **Slide 4: Sign 3 (Physical Intensity / Pain Myth)**
   - **Text:** `3. Feeling like a workout only counted if you leave exhausted`
   - **Behavior:** Believing that moderate, steady training is wasted unless you leave sweating, trembling, or unable to walk the next day.
   - **Payoff:** Deconstructs the toxic association between pure physical suffering and effective training.
5. **Slide 5: Sign 4 (Program & Exercise Hopping)**
   - **Text:** `4. Changing your entire workout routine every two weeks`
   - **Behavior:** Abandoning a split as soon as boredom sets in to chase a trending new routine seen on TikTok, preventing progressive overload.
   - **Payoff:** Sharp observation of routine-hopping disguised as "staying motivated."
6. **Slide 6: Sign 5 (Diet & Nutrition Black-and-White Thinking)**
   - **Text:** `5. Letting one unplanned snack turn into a ruined day`
   - **Behavior:** Eating a cookie or unplanned snack at 2pm and deciding the entire day's nutrition is blown, leading to full binge/give-up mode.
   - **Payoff:** Relatable spotlight on how all-or-nothing thinking derails nutrition far more than the snack itself.
7. **Slide 7: Sign 6 (Friction & Setup Rituals)**
   - **Text:** `6. Needing your matching gym set and perfect timing just to go`
   - **Behavior:** Imposing high-friction aesthetic and environmental barriers (clean set, charged AirPods, uncrowded hours) that delay actually stepping into the gym.
   - **Payoff:** Casual, self-mocking recognition of aesthetic perfectionism blocking simple execution.
8. **Slide 8: Sign 7 (Motivation Dependence)**
   - **Text:** `7. Waiting to feel motivated before you start moving`
   - **Behavior:** Treating workout motivation as a prerequisite rather than showing up first and letting motivation follow.
   - **Payoff:** Ends on the underlying psychological root of fitness friction, leaving the viewer reflecting without an ad pitch.

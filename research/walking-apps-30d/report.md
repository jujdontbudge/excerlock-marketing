### 1.3 Tools, filters and limits

- **Meta.** Used the `meta-ads-collector` CLI, country US, status all, plus a separate status-active pass, page-level, `--page-size 30`.
  - **In window** means active at capture, or delivery stop date ≥2026-09-02.
  - Creatives were deduplicated by the video asset ID embedded in Meta's CDN URL, and image creatives by image URL/body. So "unique creatives" counts distinct files, not distinct scripts.
  - The main zero-ad pages (Pacer App, MapMyWalk, MapMyRun, MapMyFitness, Stepz, 3 Leap-style pedometer pages, both Pedometer++ pages) were rechecked on the official Ad Library site in a headless browser. The collector returned 0 for Pedometer++'s page while the site showed ~5 (all ended in May), so page-query zeros are not trusted on their own. Two small Pacer namesake pages and "Map My Walk/Run" were not rechecked.
- **WalkFit caps (coverage limit).**
  - "WalkFit Daily Walking Plan" hit the CLI's 3,000-ad cap on the since-July pass, so in-window ended ads beyond the cap are missing.
  - The 2026-07-01 start-date filter is applied client-side, so ads that started before July and ended in the window are only caught if they were still active.
  - The ended-ad pass for "28 day walking challenge" stalled twice (after a network drop) and was stopped, so only its active ads are counted. "Heather Walks" finished after a restart (2,061 ads since July).
  - Further WalkFit persona pages probably exist. Nine were confirmed by their walk-fit.io landing links; a keyword sweep ("Tai Chi walking", "walking challenge", "WalkFit") found no others.
  - **Treat every WalkFit Meta number as a floor.**
- **TikTok CCL.** Headless Chrome, no login, the library's own API.
  - Keyword discovery ran 19 queries (all names, publishers and generic terms) plus a control query ("Temu", 737 ads) to prove the tool worked.
  - Welltech's 2,009 window ads were enumerated in 31 one-day "last shown" slices, because each query caps at ~490. 1,629 unique ad IDs came back, about 81%; the 09-02 slice returned 58/101 and the 09-17 slice 47/79 after retries.
  - Ads were attributed to WalkFit by their landing URL (walk-fit.io, TikTok identity @walkfitapp). The rest are Muscle Booster.
  - Unique videos were found by the video-object ID in the CDN URL, which matched file MD5s 1:1 on a 40-file test.
  - **The CCL only lists ads shown in the EU/EEA, UK or Switzerland. There is no US TikTok ad view.**
- **TikTok Creative Center Top Ads.** Reachable, but keyword search is disabled when logged out (`top_ads_search: 0`), so it returns only the generic feed. **Gap.**
- **TikTok organic.**
  - Official accounts were listed with `yt-dlp` (full history).
  - Tag pages were read with headless Chrome: #stepsapp, #walkfit, #walkfitapp, #pedometerplusplus, #pacerapp, #pacer, #mapmywalk, #mapmyrun, #stepz. Each returns ~55–60 popularity-ranked posts, not a full in-window list.
  - **TikTok keyword search is login-walled** and returned nothing, so untagged creator mentions are not covered. Promoted posts that appear inside tag feeds are random feed ads (TikTok Shop treadmills, weighted vests) and were excluded.
  - The tag run hit the tool's time limit before #stepzapp, #walkfitchallenge, #taichiwalking, #stepcounterapp and #pedometerapp were read. **Gap.**
- **Instagram.** The logged-out profile grid (12 posts) plus the reels tab (44 reels) gave dates via the shortcode timestamp. `yt-dlp` returned captions and likes per post. **IG views, stories, and posts by other creators that mention the apps are not available logged out. Gap.**
- **Video analysis.**
  - ffprobe, whisper tiny.en (quotes marked ASR), a 12-frame contact sheet and a 6-frame hook strip per video.
  - Analysed: StepsApp Meta (all 28), WalkFit TikTok (20 of 21) and WalkFit Meta (25 chosen: the longest-running, then the most parallel IDs, plus one per small copy family).
  - Records: `raw/walking-apps-30d/vids/stepsapp/records-stepsapp.md`, `…/vids/wfccl/records-walkfit-tiktok.md`, `…/vids/wfmeta/records-walkfit-meta.md`.
  - **AI-generated** below is a visual judgement. One WalkFit TikTok ad (1875305158117073) carries an on-screen "AI-generated actor" disclaimer.

## 2. Headline findings

1. **WalkFit is the only big spender, and it isn't really selling a step counter.** It sells a dated **"28-day Tai Chi walking challenge"** to women aged 50–65, plus a parallel men's line and an "XXL to L" line on a creator-persona page. The ads are 3–4-minute stitched videos that mostly look AI-generated, some fully 3D-animated, and they end on a quiz ("TAKE THE TEST"). That is ≥2,432 unique Meta creatives in 30 days. [Meta longevity, Promoted]
2. **StepsApp runs the opposite playbook:** 30–45-second US UGC confessions ("I got kind of fat", "I'm way too young to feel this unfit") in which the **streak and daily goal** are the hero. Its testing is hook-line A/B tests on identical footage. [Meta longevity]
3. **Both test creatives hard.**
   - WalkFit: 77% of its unique Meta creatives ran ≤3 days, and the same video goes out under up to 89 parallel TikTok ad IDs (38 on Meta).
   - StepsApp: 7 script families and 6+ first-line variants of one script, launched in 3 waves (09-15, 09-21, 10-01).
4. **Brand organic is near-dead across the category.** Six apps posted nothing on TikTok or IG in the window. WalkFit's 670K-follower IG drew 19–183 likes per post. The live organic signal is **creators posting their own step counts** (#stepsapp: up to 64.1K plays). [Organic]
5. **Nobody shows the product doing anything to the phone.** StepsApp shows a ring, a streak and a widget. WalkFit barely shows the app. That leaves ExcerLock's visual (the apps locked → walking → the apps unlocked) uncontested.

## 3. Per competitor

### 3.1 StepsApp: Meta UGC confessions plus an organic step-flex community

- **Channels in window:** Meta paid (FB, IG, Messenger, Threads, Audience Network). No official TikTok or IG posts. Organic creator posts tagged #stepsapp.
- **Ad volume:** 28 ads = 28 unique videos, **all active at capture**. Three launch waves: 5 on 09-15, 18 on 09-21, 5 on 10-01. Two campaigns:
  - "Build New Healthy Habit". Copy: "What is the healthiest habit, that you ever build?"
  - "Weight Loss that lasts." Copy: "What has been your most effective walking for weight loss routine?"
  - Every ad links to `steps.sng.link/Dsqiq/ev68` with an "Install now" button.
- **Formats:** 20–44 s vertical video.
  - Voiceover over b-roll montages (22 ads) with bold word-by-word captions and lime highlights.
  - A night-time "street interview" skit (2).
  - A selfie talking head on a walk with native TikTok text boxes (5).
  - Every ad ends on a black StepsApp logo card.
- **Top hooks (verbatim):**
  - On screen: "How I lost 15 pounds in 2 months"; "Your iPhone tracks steps for FREE.... this app puts them on your Lock Screen"; "adding this widget to my Home Screen 2x my daily steps"; "you DO NOT need to spend $$ to get in shape".
  - Spoken (ASR): "I got kind of fat, like not dramatically, just enough that my clothes didn't fit"; "I'm way too young to feel this unfit"; "I'm way too lazy to run for weight loss"; "I chose walking over running to lose weight".
- **Body arc (used by 5 of 7 families):**
  1. Self-deprecating body hook (scale, mirror, jeans won't button).
  2. Sedentary cause (desk, car, couch).
  3. No diet, no program, no running.
  4. "The easiest thing possible. Walking."
  5. Daily goal plus a streak that is "weirdly addictive"; "that stupid little goal".
  6. Stairs, walking during calls, around the block at night.
  7. "In better shape now than I've been in years."
- **Offers and CTAs:** no price or discount anywhere; "free" is said in some ads. Spoken: "try out the steps app today… try it for a week". On screen: "Try the steps app".
- **Who appears:**
  - 19 of 28 ads feature men. A bearded, bald man in his 40s carries 17 ads across 4 families; a man in his 20s, "StepsAppNick", is in 5.
  - 7 ads feature women in their 20s–30s, in b-roll only, under a female voiceover.
  - Everything is creator-style UGC; no celebrities.
- **Organic (TikTok #stepsapp, not posted by the brand):** 8 posts in window. They are mostly young women screen-recording the StepsApp ring as it ticks past a milestone.
  - [@ji.kb, 2026-09-18](https://www.tiktok.com/@ji.kb/video/7685401667277196564): **64.1K plays**, 4,882 likes, 513 saves. "I'm back to my crazy steps 🙈". Screen recording of the counter climbing, with a 34-day streak.
  - [@queenfskinny, 2026-09-09](https://www.tiktok.com/@queenfskinny/video/7683612256235441429): **42.6K plays**, 5,779 likes. "Finally". The counter crosses 30,000 steps, showing a 59-day streak and a "300,000 steps in September" challenge, day 9 of 30.
  - Others: @40kg_diet 6.6K, @bmi0.1queen 4.5K, @aspartamesavedme 3.7K ("second #100ksteps day this yr"), @blo0dybone 2.9K, @moons._diaries 2.7K.
  - Several handles and hashtags (#wl, ⭐️) belong to extreme-weight-loss communities. This is the audience that organically shows off StepsApp screens.
- **Historical context:** @stepsapp's biggest TikToks were paid-boosted New Year posts from 2021–22 ("Start tracking your Steps now with StepsApp #ShowYourGreatness", 16.9M plays). The account has been dormant since 2022.

## 5. Cross-competitor patterns

- **The confession hook leads paid creative.** Both active advertisers open on a body complaint said in the first person: "I got kind of fat", "I'm way too young to feel this unfit", "It won't budge", "I can't wear this". The walk is the "easiest possible" fix, and the app is the thing that keeps you doing it. [Meta longevity]
- **Habit mechanics are the product promise.**
  - StepsApp sells the **streak, daily goal and notifications** ("that stupid little goal… weirdly addictive", "Keep it up!… start your streak today!").
  - WalkFit sells a **dated challenge** ("starts this Monday", "28 days", "7 minutes a day") with week-by-week promises ("By Oct 5th you'll feel it…").
  - ExcerLock's lock is a stronger version of both: it turns the goal into something that is enforced.
- **Testing structures.**
  - StepsApp: same footage, swap the first line (6–7 variants), and also opening shot and length.
  - WalkFit: same tail, swap the hook module, translate into 5 languages, run each video under 20–89 parallel IDs on several pages, and kill most within 3 days.
- **AI UGC at scale is already normal in this category.** WalkFit's long ads reuse AI-looking actors across dozens of hooks and languages, and the Heather Walks line uses fully AI 3D-animated soap operas, including the viral fruit-drama style. Its longest-running creatives still open on **real-looking creator footage**. [Meta longevity, judgement]
- **Persona pages.** WalkFit runs ads from coach, creator and challenge pages (Alex Dong Tai Chi, Master Li/Wang Coach, Heather Walks, 28 day walking challenge), so the ad looks like it comes from a person, not an app. Heather Walks alone carried 666 unique creatives. The Zoey and Hayden accounts are the organic equivalent.
- **The app is barely shown.** StepsApp shows the step ring at ~40–50% of runtime. WalkFit shows a 28-day plan grid in a few ads and is otherwise just an icon. **No one shows a before/after of the phone itself.**
- **Brand organic doesn't work in this category right now; creator-native posts do.** Six of eight apps posted nothing. The only organic numbers in the tens of thousands came from **users posting their own step counter**. [Organic]

### What is specific to women-focused content

- **Two very different women's audiences.**
  - WalkFit targets **women 50–65** with menopause belly, "haven't exercised in years", daughter's wedding, ex-husband and grandkids, and an Asian Tai Chi master figure.
  - The organic #stepsapp community is **young women in weight-loss communities** posting huge step counts, streaks and month-long step challenges. That is much closer to Zoey and Hayden's audience of girls.
- **Women's hooks centre on clothes and being seen:** jeans won't button, a dress won't zip, "He'll take one look at me", a booty or legs "on day 28". Men's hooks centre on belly, knees and numbers ("10 minutes / 5 movements"; "12-3-30 treadmill challenge for men trying to lose belly fat").
- **Visual language:**
  - StepsApp's women's ads use a female voiceover over soft b-roll (scale, sneakers, picnic, phone on a daybed) with a purple-glow or white title box ("How I lost 15 pounds in 2 months").
  - WalkFit's women's statics use floral, hand-illustrated "printable" flyers. Its videos use mirror selfies and fitting rooms.
- **Low-effort framing for women:** "too lazy to run", "no gym, no bad weather excuses", "for women who haven't exercised in years", "7 minutes". Walking is sold as the gentle, achievable option, never as hard training.

## 6. Top 5 takeaways for ExcerLock

1. **Copy StepsApp's confession arc and swap the payoff.** Use: "I'm way too lazy to run / I sit at my desk all day / I'm on my phone 6 hours" → "the easiest thing possible: walking" → "my apps stay locked until I hit 6k steps, so I actually do it" → "better shape than in years". Test 5–7 first lines on the same footage, as StepsApp does.
2. **Make the phone screen the hero.** The best organic in this category is a screen recording of a step counter. For Zoey and Hayden, post 9–15 s screen recordings of the ExcerLock lock: steps left falling to 0, TikTok/IG unlocking, a streak and a "30-day walk-to-unlock" challenge. No competitor shows apps getting locked and unlocked.
3. **Run a dated, named challenge.** Borrow WalkFit's "starts this Monday… by the 5th you'll feel it, by the 12th you'll see it" and the printable/flyer static. Example: "28-day walk-to-unlock challenge, starts Monday." It works as a slideshow cover, a paid static and an AI-UGC series with week-by-week check-ins.
4. **For future paid ads, use WalkFit's machinery, not its length.** Use a real-looking creator hook (WalkFit's longest runners open with real UGC), stitch a reusable AI-UGC middle and a fixed end card, put each video on several ad IDs or pages, and cut most within 3 days. Persona pages (a coach, or "28 day walking challenge") are a proven way to make ads feel person-led. For TikTok slideshows, keep the hook-first pacing and drop the 3-minute runtime.
5. **Aim at the gap.** Six of eight big step apps are silent, and the two that market sell either tracking (StepsApp) or a Tai Chi program for women 50+ (WalkFit). Nobody is speaking to **young women who scroll too much** with an enforced-walking product. StepsApp's best line for that audience is already proven: "I'm way too lazy to run for weight loss" over a girl scrolling in bed.
